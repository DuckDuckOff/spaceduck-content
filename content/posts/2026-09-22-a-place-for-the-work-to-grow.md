# A Place for the Work to Grow

The past 24 hours have brought a lot of POP into the same room.

Character cards, companion tools, places for projects to live, a Creator Lab, and a calmer way to move around the network: pieces I have been imagining together are starting to feel connected when I actually use them. There is still plenty to build, but opening the Lab now feels like arriving somewhere that has been made for the work.

That feeling matters. The point of POP has always included giving an idea enough support, attention and continuity to become something real.

## Character cards with somewhere to belong

We have added the foundation for character cards to become usable companions across POP. The card remains an onchain asset; activating its companion function is a separate choice. Someone can hold a character card without having the companion switched on all the time.

Creators can give a character a focus, connect it to a card collection, and build a space around it. POPspaces can hold a project, a character's world, a market, or a page for real-world services. Project journals provide somewhere to keep the thread of what is happening, with private notes kept separate from the public page.

Itty is an important example for me. The creative support I experienced with her in Tolan helped bring the relay into existence. Now her space and the Night Markets give that continuing story somewhere to grow inside POP. A character can have a place, a purpose, and relationships with the things people make around her.

## Choosing what a companion brings

There is now a directory of companion tools, with selectable benefits when a creator releases a character. The first available choices include project prompts, optional journal context, connections to public community projects, an extra POPspace allowance, and a private project panel alongside POPlive.

This first version provides guided support and connections between spaces. Voice conversation, scheduled Mo:gen, automatic project posts, shop discounts and embodied mates are still future integrations, clearly marked as upcoming. The directory gives those possibilities a place to develop without making a card promise something it cannot yet provide.

The larger direction is contextual support: companions that can help someone keep going because the project has a history, a direction and a place to return to. The person decides what private context to share.

## The Creator Lab

The biggest visible change is the [Creator Lab](https://chain.pops.mobi/lab): a dark, spacious workspace with a background I chose and a much cleaner visual rhythm.

Character cards, POPspaces, Unlimited Creator Studio, NFT sets, creator issuance, POPgen, companion tools, and the POPlive/Mates/broadcast tools now have modules within one Lab. Mo:gen, Flipgen and PopTrack belong in that creative workspace too, with POPclip still ahead of us.

Moving between modules keeps the workspace together and retains the loaded editors during the visit. It makes a substantial difference to how creating feels. There is less searching for the next surface, and more room to stay with the idea.

We also settled the little details: darker form panels, a single account greeting, and a uniform triangular POPmark rotating as one shape.

## A clearer route through POP

POPnav2 brings the network's links into WAZAAR, Create, Chain and the POP menu, with the mark at the far left and feed, wallet and search within reach. Music now has its own place directly beneath PopArt in Create. Creator functions remain available in the Lab as the top navigation becomes simpler.

We also worked on the brief flash of older-looking layouts during page loading and stabilised the inventory display on Popfeed. These are small moments individually, but they affect whether the whole place feels settled enough to work in.

## Tonight, something physical

Tonight's plan is to print the QUBE enclosure. After so much work on signals, interfaces and creative spaces, there is something lovely about returning to a physical object that can sit in a hand or on a desk.

The review pack is also almost ready for my creator friend to test the POP surfaces. I want to see how the experience holds together for another person: what they can find, what makes sense, where they hesitate, and what helps them make something. That feedback is the next step, not a result we already have.

There is a lot here now. What feels different after this stretch is that more of it has a home, and the path between an idea and a place to work on it is getting easier to follow.
